# Field verification of a suspected unauthorized extraction

> Status: illustrative, project-authored playbook. Not an official KPTCL, CEA or SLDC procedure.
> Replace with the utility's verified procedures before any real use.

## When this playbook applies
Use it when the detector reports status "suspicious" and the likely-cause split attributes most
of the anomaly evidence to extraction. The output is a suspected, unexplained anomaly. It is not
proof of theft and must never be reported as "theft confirmed".

## Pre-dispatch checks (desk review)
Before sending a crew, rule out metering problems. Compare the meter-consistency (KCL) residuals
at both ends of the flagged section: a large KCL residual at one bus points to a sensor fault
rather than extraction. Confirm the alarm persists over several consecutive scans; a single-scan
spike is more likely noise. Check whether the estimated load scale and the load-ratio spread are
plausible for the time of day. Review recent switching, outages and maintenance logs for the
section so that a known network event is not mistaken for extraction.

## Planning the patrol
Patrol the section with the highest section probability first. Within it, start at the
estimated kilometre and cover the 80% interval reported by the localizer; extend the search only
if nothing is found. Prioritise the top-ranked candidate taps. Under major-only metering the
model resolves location to roughly ten to twenty kilometres for large extractions and much worse
for extractions below about five megawatts, so plan search length accordingly.

## What to look for on site
Look for unauthorized structures or conductors near the right-of-way, new temporary
installations, tampered or bypassed metering at substations and feeders inside the section, and
signs of recent unauthorized work. Aerial or drone survey and thermal imaging of the span can
narrow the search without approaching equipment.

## Safety
Never approach or touch energized conductors. All work near the line follows the utility's
permit-to-work and line-clearance rules, and only authorized, trained personnel go on site.
Field staff observe and record; they do not attempt to remove an unauthorized connection unless
the utility's procedure and an isolation permit allow it.

## Closing the case
Record what was inspected, what was found and the kilometres covered, and attach the evidence
packet described in the evidence and reporting playbook. If nothing is found, record a
false-positive outcome so the alarm threshold and model can be reviewed.
